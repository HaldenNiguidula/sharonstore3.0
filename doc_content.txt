In the modern globalized economy, the transition from traditional, manual operations to digitized frameworks is no longer a luxury but a competitive necessity. To address these global operational challenges, the United Nations established the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), specifically highlighting SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), which emphasizes the need to upgrade the technological capabilities of enterprises, and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), which targets the reduction of retail waste and commercial spoilage (United Nations, 2024). Globally, enterprises handling fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) are shifting toward data-driven inventory management to prevent overstocking and minimize financial losses from expired products (F1000Research, 2024).In the Philippines, local grocery stores operating within bustling public markets play a vital role in sustaining community economies. However, many of these small and medium enterprises (SMEs) still rely heavily on traditional pen-and-paper methods for their daily operations (Asian Development Bank, 2025; Department of Trade and Industry, 2024). The manual recording of hundreds of daily transactions involving canned goods, dry commodities, and household essentials is highly prone to human error. Without a centralized system to track fast-moving items and monitor approaching expiration dates, these traditional businesses often suffer from inaccurate stock counts, leading to unexpected stockouts of high-demand products and the costly disposal of expired goods (Villanueva & Reyes, 2025).This operational gap is highly evident at Sharon Store, a retail grocery business located in the Pampang Public Market, Angeles City, Pampanga. Operating since 2017 with a workforce of 8 operational employees and 2 managing owners, the store currently manages its extensive daily inventory and sales transactions through physical logbooks. This manual approach makes it nearly impossible for the management to instantly determine the exact quantity of available goods or accurately predict future consumer demand for daily staples. Consequently, the business faces challenges in optimizing its wholesale purchasing, often relying on physical estimations rather than concrete data, which ultimately hampers its operational efficiency and economic growth (RSIS International, 2026). To resolve these critical inefficiencies, this project proposes the development of a Web-Based Inventory and Sales Forecasting System tailored specifically for Sharon Store. By transitioning from a manual recording process to an automated digital database equipped with a Point of Sale (POS) transaction interface and a Business Intelligence Dashboard, the management will gain real-time visibility into their stock levels. Furthermore, the integration of a sales forecasting module will empower the owner to make data-driven purchasing decisions, ensuring that essential grocery products are consistently available while drastically reducing the financial hemorrhage associated with unsold, expired merchandise.Statement of the ProblemThe study seeks to address the issues arising from the continued use of manual inventory and record-keeping processes at Sharon Store. Specifically, it aims to answer the following problemsThe inventory process is inefficient due to reliance on manual procedures, resulting in data redundancy and inaccurate stock levels.The current manual system increases the likelihood of financial losses due to spoilage (overstocking) or missed sales opportunities (understocking) caused by the lack of predictive forecasting tools.Sensitive transaction data and historical sales records are stored in physical logbooks, making data management vulnerable to loss, damage, or unauthorized access.The absence of an integrated Business Intelligence dashboard limits the management's ability to visualize real-time sales performance and identify market trends for immediate decision-making.Objectives of the StudyThe general objective of the study is to develop and implement a Web-Based Inventory and Sales Forecasting System with Business Intelligence Dashboard for Sharon Store that streamlines inventory processes, improves the accuracy of sales predictions, and enhances data visualization for the management of fast-moving consumer goods, dry commodities, and household essentials at the Pampang Public Market. Specifically, it aims:To design and implement an automated inventory module that reduces the time and effort required to monitor stock levels compared to the current manual system.To integrate a sales forecasting module that improves the accuracy and reliability of predicting future demand based on historical data.To establish a secure digital storage and retrieval mechanism with regular backups to protect sensitive business transaction records.To provide an integrated Business Intelligence (BI) Dashboard accessible to management, enabling them to visualize sales trends, fast-moving items, and critical stock levels in real-time.Scope of the StudyThis study focuses on the design, development, and implementation of a Web-Based Inventory and Sales Forecasting System for Sharon Store, specifically located in the Pampang Public Market, Angeles City, Pampanga. The study emphasizes improving data accuracy, forecasting reliability, and security compared to the current manual processes. The implementation and evaluation of the system will occur within one academic year, focusing on its functionality, usability, and security, as well as stakeholder satisfaction.The modules covered by the study are arranged according to the users of the system. For the System Administrator or Owner, the system includes a Business Intelligence Dashboard Module, which is a graphical interface that allows the owner to visualize real-time sales performance, track fast-moving grocery items, and monitor overall inventory health. It also features a Sales Forecasting Module, a data analysis tool that processes historical sales data to predict future consumer demand for daily staples and generate optimal restocking recommendations. Furthermore, an Inventory Management Module provides full control to add, update, and categorize grocery products (such as canned goods, dry commodities, and household essentials), and strictly track stock levels in pieces, packs, or kilograms.For the Staff, the system provides a Point of Sale (POS) Module integrated with barcode scanning capabilities. This serves as a rapid transaction interface used to scan items, instantly record daily sales, process customer purchases, and automatically deduct sold grocery items from the active inventory to ensure fast checkout speeds. Delimitations of the StudyTo maintain strict alignment with Information Systems principles and ensure project feasibility, the system boundaries explicitly exclude several key areas. First, regarding excluded business operations, the system operates strictly as a specialized inventory and sales management tool; it completely excludes administrative tasks such as employee payroll processing, official BIR tax generation, and online customer e-commerce or delivery tracking. Second, concerning procurement, while the system monitors stock levels and provides data-driven recommendations for restocking, it fundamentally requires manual procurement. The system will not autonomously order goods; the management must still physically purchase from wholesale suppliers to negotiate prices and inspect product conditions. Furthermore, in terms of hardware integration, the system is strictly limited to standard USB or Bluetooth barcode scanners to expedite the POS checkout process. The system explicitly excludes integration with other proprietary Point of Sale (POS) peripherals such as automated receipt printers, electronic cash drawers, or physical anti-theft security tags.  Third, regarding physical enforcement, the system is decoupled from anti-theft hardware. It does not integrate with physical security tags, CCTV cameras, or hardware sensors to monitor shoplifting within the grocery store. Finally, concerning forecasting constraints, the predictive module relies exclusively on historical sales data. It does not autonomously account for sudden external anomalies such as supplier shortages, rapid economic inflation, or localized panic buying during natural calamities.Conceptual FrameworkFigure 1. Descriptive research is appropriate when the goal is to understand the current state of a system, group, or process: what is happening now. Employing a descriptive method is practical and relevant for this project because the proposed software system must fit the existing business operations of Sharon Store. This design will enable the researchers to capture an accurate baseline of how the current manual grocery operations run, identifying existing pain points and objectives before attempting to implement the web-based system. This baseline will be captured through interactive approaches, such as interviews with the store management, and unobtrusive methods, such as direct observation of the checkout workflow and reviewing existing physical logbooks.Systems Development MethodologyFigure 2. Agile MethodologyAgile Development MethodologyThe researchers will utilize the Agile methodology for this capstone project (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).  Agile is an iterative approach to software development that delivers work in small, manageable increments called sprints. It emphasizes collaboration, flexibility, and continuous feedback, allowing teams to adapt to changing requirements throughout the project. Agile was selected because the system requires regular user feedback from the Sharon Store management regarding the Point of Sale (POS) interface and inventory workflows. By utilizing short cycles and constant communication, the system can receive continuous feedback for further improvements and refinements, ensuring that the final checkout and forecasting modules perfectly align with the fast-paced nature of the public market.Requirement & AnalysisThis phase begins with establishing definitive system requirements using data acquired from structured interviews with the two (2) store owners and eight (8) operational employees, combined with direct observations of the current manual logbook workflow. The needs analysis maps out these physical data bottlenecks and converts them into technical specifications. The technical analysis takes into account software aspects tailored for a local deployment, selecting an offline-first XAMPP environment (Apache server), PHP via PDO for backend operations, HTML5, CSS3, Vanilla JavaScript, and Bootstrap 5.3.0 for a highly responsive user interface, and MySQL for relational database management. It also considers strict hardware requirements, ensuring that the system interfaces seamlessly with desktop/laptop terminals (minimum 8GB RAM) and standard USB or Bluetooth barcode scanners to support rapid front-end cashier processing.System DesignAfter obtaining all of the requirements, the system's technical architecture is meticulously planned out. Visual modeling tools are utilized to generate data flow diagrams (DFD Level 0 and Level 1), use case diagrams, and localized database designs that clearly define data trajectories and user permission boundaries. This stage entails designing a secure database structure across specific relational tables (such as tbl_inventory, tbl_transactions, and tbl_forecasts) to systematically handle product categories, stock counts (pieces, packs, or kilograms), real-time low-stock levels, and approaching expiration dates. User interface mockups are structured to prioritize large, clear text displays and intuitive navigation buttons, ensuring cashiers can execute checkouts swiftly during peak market rushes without causing customer delays.ImplementationThe system's actual coding begins during the implementation phase. Based on the logical rules set during the design stage, the developers construct the application modules iteratively. Primary development focus is channeled into programming the comprehensive System Admin (Owner) Module—including the real-time data visual charts for the Business Intelligence Dashboard, the predictive sales forecasting script based on historical transaction logs, and the database backup and audit trail tools. Concurrently, the Staff (Cashier) Module is coded, focusing heavily on embedding standard barcode-scanning scripts into the Point of Sale (POS) checkout line and establishing automated, real-time inventory deductions upon finalized sales.TestingDuring the testing phase, the system is evaluated for processing errors and its operational efficiency is confirmed. To ensure that all core features such as real-time low-stock warnings, instant total amount calculations, and historical demand data projections work flawlessly under load, a multi-tiered testing methodology is employed. This includes isolated Unit Testing of code blocks, Integration Testing of connected database tables, and User Acceptance Testing (UAT) conducted directly by the workforce within the Sharon Store locale. Any data mismatch, scanner latency, or calculation anomalies identified during these rounds are completely resolved before final system verification.DevelopmentThroughout the active timeline, the development phase consists of continuously improving the system infrastructure based on incremental layout evaluations and stakeholder feedback. The application is built on a local server backend managed via XAMPP, which handles critical server-side functions like data entry tracking, hardcopy report generation requests, and sales forecasting analytics. Front-end frameworks like Bootstrap 5.3.0 and Font Awesome 6.5.0 are utilized to maintain a clean, user-friendly, and modern visual display. Following the deployment of early test increments, new feature tweaks and dashboard optimizations are pushed out based directly on manager and cashier evaluations, ensuring the system perfectly fits the fast-paced nature of a public market grocery store. MaintenanceOngoing maintenance is required once the software is fully implemented at the store locale to address operational bugs, secure historical data, or adjust parameters over time. Maintenance protocols guarantee that the web application will continue to remain secure, functionally stable, and pertinent to the business's daily operations. This phase includes monitoring database indexing efficiency as transaction counts grow, rolling out localized bug patches, verifying that daily automated database backups function smoothly, and updating system thresholds for low-stock triggers as store inventory expands.ReviewAfter the system has been in continuous deployment for a designated period, a formal review step is executed to evaluate its overall performance impact against the old paper-based methods. Quantitative and qualitative feedback is gathered from both technical evaluators (I.T. Experts) and end-users (Management and Staff) using evaluation tools structured around the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model. This step measures actual efficiency gains, such as checkout transaction speed improvements, the accuracy of generated restocking recommendations, and general user learnability. Noted areas of optimization are cataloged as data references for future system updates.LaunchThe system is formally launched for live business operations following final data clearing and environment optimization. During this stage, the manual paper logbooks are retired, and the program becomes the central hub for all active retail and wholesale grocery transactions at Sharon Store. The research team provides close post-launch on-site support during the first week of implementation to monitor barcode scanner responsiveness, assist cashiers during peak market rushes, and ensure maximum system stability under actual, real-world conditions.ParticipantsParticipants of the study are individuals, groups, or organizations who are actively involved in a research project and from whom the researcher collects data to evaluate the system.
